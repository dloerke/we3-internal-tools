How we’d redesign the PancakeSwap app

What’s this? It’s a single-file, interactive scoping document from WE3 to PancakeSwap, based on our conversations with Chef Miso on 10 November. It sets out a proposed app redesign as a fixed core plus adjustable options, and shows a live price range and timeline that adjust as you play around with the scope. Its aim is to provide a rough estimation of price ranges and help us agree on a scope together. It’s not a fixed quote to accept or reject.

WE3

Must Have

The core of the project, in the order we’d run it. These steps are part of every package. This is the smallest scope that allows us to deliver designs that live up to our own standards.

  1. 1

    Understanding

    We like to read up before we ask questions.

    We’ll request your key documents to thoroughly review and get up to speed quickly so we can ask better questions in the stakeholder interviews. For example:

    • Documentation on your key features
    • Internal strategy and roadmap documents
    • Internal app feedback and support requests
    • Interviews & podcasts that reflect your strategy & goals
    • Brand guidelines
    • Figma files and the current design system
    • Earlier research, surveys or test results
    • Community feedback from Discord, Telegram and app stores
  2. 2

    Stakeholder interviews

    We’ll talk to the people who shape the app and map the perspectives to guide our decision making in the design process.

    Product OwnerChef MisoTo understand the business goals and what the redesign should achieve.
    Gamification leadChef MisoTo understand the goals of the initiative and the incentive mechanics.
    Brand OwnerChef KidsTo hear her aspirations for the brand and what should stay recognisably PancakeSwap.
    Developer or Tech LeadEngineeringTo learn about the limits of the current implementation, the tech stack and security considerations.
    DesignerTo understand the decisions that led to the PWA as it is today.
  3. 3

    Audit and competitors

    A fresh look at the current app and at what others do well.

    We review the app from first principles, guided by established usability heuristics, accessibility good practice and what we’ve learned designing other wallets and exchanges. Then we look at key competitors to spot gaps in features, visual design and usability.

    We’d start with Binance Wallet, Trust Wallet and Uniswap and adjust and amend the set with you.

  4. 4

    Goal-setting workshop

    Agreeing together on what the redesign should achieve.

    We design this workshop based on what we learned in the steps before. Together we define what we’re aiming for and prioritise the features and flows for the redesign. We come away with a prioritised scope agreed with the product lead.

    Timing

    We hold the workshop from 30 November, right after Binance Blockchain Week, so anything we pick up there feeds in as well.

  5. 5

    Visual direction

    Finding a look that fits where PancakeSwap is heading.

    We look for references that come closer to the look we’re after and support the strategic direction, across crypto, fintech, games, consumer apps and art. Together we narrow them down with moodboards and your team’s feedback, then design one key screen in a few variations so we quickly agree on look and feel using real UI.

    Along the way we develop the colour system and make font recommendations.

  6. 6

    Product design

    The key flows of the app, designed in Figma.

    Based on the chosen visual direction, we design the key flows of the app. We think through how the designs scale, so your developers can implement them and your team can extend them with confidence.

    The flows are also set up as a clickable Figma prototype, ready to test. And of course we deliver the components as a tidy Figma library, so your team can reuse them, keep screens consistent and build new features faster.

Should Have

Each option can be set from low touch to high touch, or left off. The dot marks our recommendation. With all options off, you have the Core package.

A

A. Gamification concept support

From exploring ideas to co-creating the concept with your team.

We think gamification will be central to the redesign reaching its goals, and closely tied to it. So we’d like to be more involved and co-create the concept with your team, rather than design around it.

Between them, the designers we’d staff have completed Kevin Werbach’s Gamification course at the Wharton School and have implemented gamification concepts with clients like Legend, Fomo, Delphi Digital, Adidas and Volkswagen.

B. Binance Blockchain Week support

From an interview script for your booth to our own interviews at the event.

Binance Blockchain Week in Bangkok on 28 and 29 November is a good occasion to talk to crypto users in person. At the low end, we support your booth team with an interview script; we’ve run hundreds of user interviews, so we think we can help. At the high end, Eric and/or Pierre come to Bangkok and run our own WE3-branded interviews about new wallet and DEX features, without mentioning PancakeSwap or our partnership.

On representing PancakeSwap

We heard you’d rather we don’t represent PancakeSwap at the event, and that works for us. We still think it’s a great occasion for in-person interviews. Afterwards we can compare notes: what you learned from your users, and what we learned from random crypto enthusiasts about a generic new app.

If we come, we cover part of the cost ourselves. Meeting in person at the start of a project tends to make our work and communication a lot smoother and more fun in a way that is hard to describe or quantify.

C

C. User testing and refinement

Testing the riskiest flows with real users before they’re built.

We test the riskiest flows as clickable prototypes with small groups of users who represent the prioritised persona, recruited from PancakeSwap’s alpha or friendly users. Then we refine the designs based on what we learn.

Our recommendation

A few users usually help you find the most important problems. So we recommend keeping this lean rather than testing excessively.

Low touchHigh touch

Our recommendation: 2/5

D

D. Platform-native design

Specific adjustments for Android and iOS.

This ranges from the necessary adjustments for each platform, to small changes in visual style so the app feels at home on Android and iOS, all the way to a separate design per platform.

Our recommendation

We’d recommend against the high end for now. Fully separate designs mean maintaining three different apps, which is too early at this stage.

Low touchHigh touch

Our recommendation: 1/5

E

E. Website extension

If the app moves too far from the website visually, the two will feel inconsistent, which can affect user trust, so you should plan resources for Q1 to align the design styles.

At the low end, we deliver Figma components your designers can reuse and adapt for the website. A step up, we design a single key screen to stress-test the new direction on desktop. At the high end, we redesign the main parts of the website, which would work well as a follow-up project.

Our recommendation

We don’t think we need to do this in this project. The Figma components should get your designers quite far and will be tidy enough to extend. If we enjoy working together, we can always follow up.

Low touchHigh touch

Our recommendation: 1/5

F

F. Motion design

At least one signature moment, designed with a motion designer.

Together with you we pick a key moment in the app and design it with an expert motion designer, documented so your developers can build it.

Our recommendation

For now, a key moment or two. Small refinements to what’s already there, based on motion best practice, can deliver an outsized return in the perception of your app.

Low touchHigh touch

Our recommendation: 2/5

G

G. Copywriting

Clearer language for people who are new to DeFi.

Our expert copywriter helps make the LP features easier to understand for people new to DeFi, to increase conversions and lower the barrier to getting started. Plain language helps people who have never heard of zap mode, fee tiers, or what a narrow price strategy implies.

This is especially worth it if you see an opportunity to bring Binance users into self-custody. After the hot-wallet breach in October, this may be a good moment.

Our recommendation

For this to work, it needs to carry through to the website, and it involves more stakeholders. So it’s probably worthwhile as a follow-up project, after we’ve proven our value in the first project.

Low touchHigh touch

Our recommendation: off for now

H

H. Design QA

Reviewing what’s being built against the designs.

Sometimes this is necessary, but we mostly end up pointing at the designs. There’s a lot of hand-holding, and it’s probably not the best use of our time together.

Our recommendation

With some developers this isn’t necessary at all, with others it’s a very large task that requires a lot of time and patience. However, this is a lot more economically sensible to do with in-house designers, so in your case we recommend against it.

If you want it, let’s talk and we’ll add it.
